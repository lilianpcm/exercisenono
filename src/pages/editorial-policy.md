Every guide on Exercise No No is researched from reputable sources, written in plain English, and checked before it is published. This page explains how.

## Who writes the guides

Guides are published by the **Exercise No No Editorial Team**. We are not doctors or physical therapists, and we do not give medical advice. Our goal is to explain common exercises clearly and safely for people working out at home.

We use AI tools to help research and draft our content. Every guide is then checked against the sources listed on the page and reviewed before it goes live.

## Our sources

We rely on recognized health and fitness institutions, such as the National Institute on Aging, the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC), the NHS, the American College of Sports Medicine (ACSM) and the Mayo Clinic. Every guide lists the sources it used at the end of the page.

## Safety first

- When guidance is unclear or sources disagree, we choose the more cautious option.
- We never claim that an exercise cures, treats or prevents a condition.
- Guides include a reminder to check with a doctor before starting a new routine, and to stop if something hurts.

## Updates and corrections

Each guide shows the date it was published and the date it was last updated. We review guides when sources change or when readers point out something unclear. Found a mistake? Write to {{ site.contact_email }} and we will check it.

## Free guides and printables

The exercise guides on this site are free. Some pages show part of a routine; the complete routine, organized as a printable chart with a step-by-step guide and video demos, is sold as a printable PDF in our [Etsy shop](https://theshopcurator.etsy.com). Product links are clearly marked.
